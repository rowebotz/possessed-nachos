Quantum Superposition (Animated)

This SVG animates two coherent waves, |ψ₁⟩ (blue) and |ψ₂⟩ (red). Their linear combination |ψ⟩ = α|ψ₁⟩ + β|ψ₂⟩ (purple) shows how relative weights and phase determine interference. The plot is illustrative: amplitudes are drawn as waves in space x and evolve with time t; the purple curve is the instantaneous superposed amplitude.

Tip: Set |α| = |β| = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707 and vary φ to see constructive/destructive interference in the purple curve.

What you are seeing

  • Linear combination: The purple curve is αψ₁(x,t) + βψ₂(x,t). Changing |α|, |β| scales each component; changing φ rotates their relative phase.
  • Relative phase matters: φ controls constructive/destructive interference. Global phase (shifting both equally) is unobservable.
  • Measurement probabilities: In a basis where these are orthonormal states, probabilities are |α|² and |β|², but the shape of ψ depends on φ as well.
  • Basis dependence: Superposition depends on the chosen basis. A state can look like a superposition here but be a basis vector elsewhere.
  • Decoherence: Coupling to an environment suppresses relative phase information, washing out interference and making outcomes classical-like.
